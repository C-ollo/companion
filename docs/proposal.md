# Spectrum & Waves Proposal

## Problem Statement
People with autism or intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD) can build
independent-living skills, but they often lack an accessible way to understand and track
their own progress. Skills learned in one setting also may not carry over when the
environment, tools, or context change. Together, these challenges make it harder to build
independence and apply skills across everyday life.

## Problem Identified Through Our Partner Research
Our visit to First Place highlighted two related challenges in building independent-living
skills: seeing their own skill progress, and transferring skills across different
environments.

### Gap 1: Self-tracking of skill progress
Individuals are building important independent-living skills, but they do not always have an
easy way to see and understand their progress. First Place staff collect data on residents'
skill development, but residents often rely on staff to tell them how they are doing, and
staff have limited time to summarize what each resident has learned.

### Gap 2: Transfer of skills across environments
Learning a skill in one environment does not always mean it will transfer when the
environment changes. First Place addresses this through its teaching kitchen, which uses the
same appliances as the residential apartments because, for some individuals, learning to use
one stove does not translate to using another. Most people with autism or IDD do not have
access to a setup like this, and even First Place residents will face unfamiliar appliances
when they move on, typically after two to three years.

## Proposed Solution
We are exploring Skill Passport, an AI-powered tool that helps people with autism or IDD
build independent-living skills and carry them into new places. It has two connected parts.

**Skill Passport.** People log everyday tasks by voice, text, or a quick tap, on their own or
with a support person. The AI turns these logs into a personal record that shows how much
help each skill still needs, from "with help" to "on my own," and suggests a next skill to
try.

**Point & Ask.** When someone faces an unfamiliar appliance or setting, they point their
phone at it and say what they want to do. The AI draws on the skills already in their
passport to explain what is different (for example, "This washer works like the one you've
used before, but the start button is here") and guides them one step at a time, at their own
pace. If it is unsure, it says so instead of guessing and helps the person reach someone they
trust. Completing the task adds it to their passport.

The person owns their passport and chooses who can see it: family, staff, a friend, or no
one.

Together, these features help individuals see what they can do, build on their progress, and
transfer their skills across everyday settings.
